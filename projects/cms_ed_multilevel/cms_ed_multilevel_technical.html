1. Study Objective

The study objective was to quantify the extent to which emergency department throughput varies across hospitals and states. The primary inferential question was whether meaningful between-state variation remains after adjustment for hospital type, ownership, and emergency department volume.

The modeling strategy was designed to emphasize variance partitioning rather than prediction alone. This distinction motivated the use of an empty random-intercept model, an intraclass correlation coefficient, and a comparison of state variance before and after hospital-level adjustment.

2. Data Sources

The analysis used two publicly available CMS Care Compare hospital datasets: Hospital General Information and Timely and Effective Care — Hospital. Facility ID was read as character data to preserve leading zeroes and used as the linkage key.

3. Data Audit and Analytic Cohort

The workflow deliberately separated import, data auditing, cleaning, descriptive analysis, modeling, diagnostics, and export. This separation reduced the risk of embedding untested assumptions directly into the cleaning code.

The data audit showed that CMS currently reports emergency department volume as low, medium, high, and very high. It also showed that the hospital file contains several specialized facility types. The primary population was therefore restricted to acute-care and critical-access hospitals that reported emergency services, had a plausible OP-18b value, and had complete primary model predictors.

Specialized hospitals remained in a broader processed dataset and were incorporated into a sensitivity model. This preserved transparency and allowed the primary restriction to be evaluated empirically.

5. Descriptive Analysis

Descriptive analysis examined the outcome distribution, hospital composition, throughput across hospital characteristics, and unadjusted state means. The purpose was to identify implausible values, characterize the analytic population, and assess whether state-level variation was visible before model fitting.

6. Model Specification

The primary adjusted model was specified as:

ED throughput = hospital type + ownership + ED volume + state random intercept

Let Yij denote throughput for hospital i in state j. The random-intercept model can be written as:

Yij = β0 + βXij + uj + εij

The state effects uj are assumed to follow a normal distribution with variance σ²state, while the hospital-level residuals εij have variance σ²hospital.

The empty-model ICC was calculated as:

ICC = σ²state / (σ²state + σ²hospital)

The ICC represents the proportion of unexplained outcome variation attributable to differences between states before hospital characteristics are added.

7. Primary Results

Final numerical results should be populated directly from variance_components.csv and adjusted_fixed_effects.csv. The reporting sequence should begin with the ICC, followed by the change in state variance after adjustment, and then the hospital-level coefficients.

8. Diagnostics

Residual-versus-fitted and Q-Q plots were used to assess functional form, residual spread, and approximate normality. The workflow also evaluated multicollinearity, heteroscedasticity, residual normality, and singularity using the performance package.

The final write-up should describe any visible departures from assumptions and whether they were substantial enough to affect interpretation. Diagnostics should be treated as evidence for model adequacy, not as a checklist detached from the scientific question.

9. Sensitivity Analyses

Two sensitivity analyses were planned. The first added overall hospital rating, accepting a smaller complete-case sample to determine whether the primary estimates were sensitive to this additional adjustment. The second expanded the cohort to include additional hospital types and added hospital type as a broader categorical predictor.

Results should be summarized from cohort_sensitivity_comparison.csv. The central question is not whether every coefficient is identical, but whether the main conclusions about state variation and hospital-level associations change materially.

10. Interpretation and Limitations

The random intercept captures residual differences between states after measured hospital characteristics are included. It should not be interpreted as a causal effect of state policy. State effects may reflect unmeasured workforce, market, rurality, regulatory, or healthcare-capacity differences.

The project also relies on CMS-reported hospital measures and complete-case analysis. Missingness may be informative, and the OP-18b measure summarizes hospital performance over a reporting period rather than individual patient experiences. The model describes associations at the hospital level and should not be interpreted as a patient-level causal analysis.

11. Reproducibility

The R workflow is organized into separate stages:

  1. Import
  2. Data audit
  3. Cleaning and cohort construction
  4. Descriptive analysis
  5. Multilevel modeling
  6. Diagnostics and sensitivity analyses
  7. Output export

Separating these stages makes it possible to inspect the raw data structure, record analytic decisions, and reproduce each output without concealing intermediate steps.
