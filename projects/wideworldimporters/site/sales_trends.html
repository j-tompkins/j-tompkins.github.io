Overview & Driving Question

Question: How are sales trending over time and which segments drive change? The finished report centers on a clean time‑series area chart to show overall trends, a clustered bar for segment comparisons, and a row of cards that surface key KPIs at a glance. Three slicers (date, category, region) enable quick pivots.

I prioritized readability for quick analysis. The page is intentionally dense but scannable—cards first, then trend, then breakdowns. This reflects how stakeholders review performance: glance at KPIs, scan the trend line, and then look for where changes originate (the clustered bar). Titles and tooltips are written in plain language and the color choices remain accessible.

Method & Build Notes

  • Model
    • Sales fact table related to Date, Product/Category, and Geography dimensions.
    • Cleaned date table with continuous range → drives the area trend and time slicer.
  • Measures
    • Total Sales (base)
    • Prev Period Sales & YoY/period deltas (for cards)
    • Contribution % by segment (for the clustered bar)
  • Visuals & UX
    • Cards (4): current Sales, YoY %, Units, and Avg Order (numbers rounded, consistent formatting).
    • Area chart: continuous time axis with subtle transparency to de‑emphasize noise.
    • Clustered bar: top segments by contribution; labels suppressed below threshold to reduce clutter.
    • Slicers: Date, Category, Region — horizontal layout for compactness.

Findings & Reflection

The area trend made seasonal patterns obvious; the clustered bar showed that a small set of categories explained most variance during peaks. Using cards to anchor context sped up read time, while slicers enabled quick what‑ifs without overwhelming the layout.

Lessons: keep the canvas single‑page when possible; use a small, consistent set of measures; and let a simple trend + one strong comparison visual do the heavy lifting.
