Broadband Access & the Digital Divide (California)

A Tableau Public dashboard exploring how poverty aligns with the broadband adoption gap and unserved households across California counties, using CPUC and ACS data.

Overview & Driving Question

Driving question: How does broadband adoption vary across California counties, and what does the gap between service availability and actual adoption tell us when combined with poverty and demographic characteristics?

The story that emerged: California’s last-mile availability is high (statewide unserved ≈ low), but the adoption gap is where the digital divide lives. I focused on four visuals: a filled map of poverty %, a stacked bar (offered vs. connections), a bubble chart relating poverty to adoption gap (size = connections), and a bar of unserved share. Together, they surface outliers and priority counties.

Interactive Dashboard (Tableau Public)

Method & Build Notes

Preparation

  • Extracted CPUC county PDFs (Adoption & Served Status) → Excel.
  • Pulled ACS 5-year (2023) S1701 (Poverty %) and DP05 (demographics) → CSV.
  • Built a county_master.csv with: All_HH, Households_Offered, Consumer_Connections, Adoption_Rate, Served/Unserved 25/3, Poverty_Pct.
  • Derived: Adoption_Gap = Offered − Connections, Unserved_Share = Unserved / All_HH.

Visual decisions (blue–teal)

  • Poverty filled map: context for economic hardship.
  • Stacked bar (Offered vs Connections): intuitive view of the adoption gap.
  • Bubble (Poverty × Adoption Gap, size=Connections): surfaces outliers.
  • Bar (Unserved Share): ranks coverage gaps separately from adoption.
  • KPIs: Offered vs Connections, Adoption Gap (hh), Adoption Rate.

What I learned / resolved from notes

  • Poverty vs Income: For this use case, poverty % was the sharper equity signal; income can be added later if needed.
  • GEOIDs vs County names: Used County in Tableau for readability; kept GEOIDs for QGIS joins later.
  • Legends vs hover: Kept at least one color legend visible (map/bubble). Others rely on tooltips for a clean layout.
  • Sorting & Top-N: Bars sorted by Adoption_Gap; Top-N filter used to focus when needed.

Findings & Interpretation

  • Statewide access is high, but adoption lags — the divide is behavioral/economic, not purely infrastructural.
  • High-gap, low-poverty outliers suggest non-economic barriers (awareness, device availability, digital literacy, plan affordability).
  • High-poverty, high-gap clusters align with equity priorities for targeted outreach and subsidy programs.

Limitations: adoption is from reported connections; “availability” reflects advertised service tiers (25/3). DP05 demographics retained for future expansion.
